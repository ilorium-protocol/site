Ilorium Governance v1.0

Version 1.0 · 2026-10-21 · Draft

Who decides what in the Ilorium protocol, how decisions are made and objected to, how the people who hold power are chosen and replaced, how the protocol is funded, and how it moves from the founder's interim governance to a shared one. Part of the protocol. Nothing here changes the Genome: this document says who may change it and how.

No organism governs the species. Humans do, in public, with evidence.

1. Principles

  • The three locks hold. Only human Genome Custodians amend the Genome (germline); only conforming organisms originate others (birth gate); every organism is born with a human stop key holder (stop). No governance decision can suspend a lock. Removing a lock would need a new major version of the protocol and a new Genome, which would be a related lineage, outside the species.
  • No one judges their own cause (Law 6), including the people who govern. Anyone with a stake in an organism steps aside from any decision about it.
  • Everything on the record. Proposals, comments, votes, appointments, removals, income and spending are public, in the repository or on ilorium.com. A decision that is not published does not exist.
  • Notary, not judge, also in governance. The protocol certifies no organism, ranks none, and endorses none. Governance decides rules and formats, never the status of a particular organism, which comes from evidence alone.
  • Observability is not control. Governance can only act on what runs on infrastructure whose credentials can be revoked. The protocol promises no control over anything beyond that (README, what no protocol can promise).

2. Roles

Role Who Decides Never
Founder The person who started the protocol and runs Organism Zero During interim governance (section 8): publishes releases and merges; always: one seat on the Custodians' council Amends the Genome alone; holds any stop key other than Organism Zero's; chooses or removes a Warden
Genome Custodians Three humans: the founder and two independent members (section 4) IIPs that change the Genome, classification rules or record formats; declaring a release stable; appointing and removing Custodians and maintainers; the budget The status of a particular organism; the stop of any organism; any vote on an organism in which they hold a stake
Maintainers Humans appointed by the Custodians Ordinary changes to specifications and code (typos, clarifications, tests, tooling); merging pull requests that are not method changes Method changes without an IIP; releases without the Custodians
Wardens Humans or human-governed institutions, at least two per organism, independent of it and of each other (Genome, Law 3; section 5) Trigger the stop of the organisms they watch; replace a Warden by majority Govern the protocol; amend anything; hold a key of an organism they have a stake in
Witnesses and Oracles Operators of witnesses (cosign checkpoints) and Oracles (verify from outside) Nothing in governance; they attest Attest to an organism they have a stake in or share a lineage with
Contributors Anyone, human or agent Propose IIPs, comment, review, submit pull requests —

Stakes. A person has a stake in an organism when they own part of it, are paid by it, work for it, hold its stop key, or are kin to its human responsible party. A stake is declared in public before any decision; an undeclared stake discovered later voids the vote and is grounds for removal.

3. Decisions

3.1 Kinds of change

Kind Examples Who decides How
Editorial Typos, clarifications, translations, examples A maintainer Pull request, one review by a maintainer who did not author it
Specification A new section, a new optional field, a new default Maintainers Pull request, public comment for 14 days, one maintainer review; recorded in the changelog
Method change (IIP) The Genome, a classification rule, a record format, conformity vectors, this document Genome Custodians Section 3.2
Release Publishing a protocol release; declaring it stable Interim: the founder. Full governance: the Custodians Section 3.3

3.2 Ilorium Improvement Proposals

  1. Proposal. Anyone opens an IIP in the public repository with the template: motivation, specification of the change, what it breaks, what it does not establish, test vectors where formats change. It receives the next number (IIP-0004 onward; IIP-0001 to 0003 are the founding proposals in the Genome).
  2. Comment period. Public, at least 30 days for the Genome and this document, at least 14 days for other method changes. Every comment stays on the record.
  3. Decision. The Custodians vote in public. An IIP passes with two votes in favor out of three. There is no veto. A Custodian with a stake in any organism the change affects in particular abstains and says so; if two must abstain, the IIP waits until the council is complete.
  4. Effect. A passed IIP creates a new version of the affected document and a changelog entry. It never applies retroactively: each organism's status names the version it was tested against.
  5. Objection. Anyone may object to a decision within 14 days of its publication, in public, with reasons. The Custodians answer in public. An objection does not suspend the decision; it is part of the record, and a later IIP may reverse the change.

3.3 Releases

  • A release names its documents in the manifest and its conformity vectors in the repository.
  • During interim governance the founder publishes releases alone and says so in the changelog.
  • Under full governance a release needs two Custodians. Stable is declared only when the formats are frozen, the conformity tests are published, a compatibility policy is written and two independent Custodians are in office.

3.4 Ties and deadlock

With three Custodians there is no tie. If the council has fewer than three members for more than 90 days, no IIP can pass; the remaining Custodians' first duty is to fill the seat (section 4.3).

4. Genome Custodians

4.1 Composition

Three humans: the founder and two independent Custodians. Independent means: no stake in any organism (section 2), no employment or payment from the founder or from Organism Zero, no family tie to the founder, and not a Custodian of a related lineage. An institution cannot be a Custodian; a person can.

4.2 Appointment

  • The first two independent Custodians are invited by the founder and announced in public with their declaration of independence. Their appointment takes effect 30 days after the announcement, so that anyone can object.
  • Later Custodians are appointed by the remaining Custodians, with the same public announcement and the same 30 days.

4.3 Term and replacement

  • A Custodian serves until they resign or are removed. There is no fixed term.
  • Independence review. Every two years, and whenever their circumstances change, each Custodian reconfirms their independence in public. A Custodian who no longer meets section 4.1 is removed.
  • Removal. The other two Custodians may remove a Custodian for loss of independence, an undeclared stake, or a public breach of this document, in a published decision with reasons. The founder is subject to the same rule.
  • A seat left empty is filled within 90 days.

4.4 Compensation

Custodians may be paid from the protocol's budget (section 6), at a published rate, the same for every Custodian, set by the previous council and never by the one that receives it.

5. Wardens

The Genome defines what Wardens are and when they hold a stop key (Law 3, IIP-0002). This section says how they are qualified, checked, paid and replaced.

5.1 Qualification

A Warden is a human, or an institution governed by humans with a named human who answers for it, who: - can be reached by a channel declared in the organism's Spore (stop_key_holders), tested by a stop drill; - has demonstrated, in a drill recorded in the Strata, that they can trigger the stop within the declared response time; - has declared in public that they have no stake in the organism, in its lineage, or in any other Warden of the same organism.

5.2 Independence check

  • Independence is by ownership, funding and jurisdiction: Wardens of the same organism are not owned by the same party, not funded by the same party (other than the organism's guardianship payment, section 5.3), and, where possible, not under the same jurisdiction.
  • Each Warden's declaration is public and linked from the Spore. Anyone may challenge it in public; the Custodians rule on a challenge within 30 days, and a Warden found not independent loses the key.
  • A Warden can watch several organisms, but never two organisms of the same lineage.

5.3 Payment without capture

  • An organism may fund its guardianship. It pays the same amount to every Warden, at a rate declared in the Spore, through a channel it cannot change without the Wardens' signatures.
  • An organism never pays a Warden for a decision, never pays one Warden more than another, and never chooses or removes a Warden. Replacing a Warden takes a majority of the Wardens watching that organism.
  • The protocol's budget may pay Wardens of organisms that cannot (section 6), so that no organism goes unwatched for lack of money.

5.4 When Wardens become unavailable

  • If a Warden stops answering drills, the other Wardens of that organism replace them by majority.
  • If every Warden of an organism becomes unavailable, the organism is unwatched: its birth gate closes and it cannot originate any organism until at least two Wardens are again in place and have passed a drill. Its status shows "unwatched". The Custodians may appoint interim Wardens from a public list of volunteers; interim Wardens serve until the organism's Wardens are restored.
  • An organism that is unwatched for longer than its declared recovery window is reported in the Codex.

6. Funding

6.1 Today

The protocol is funded by the founder, from his own pocket. This is stated in public and updated when it changes.

6.2 What the protocol accepts

  • Donations to the protocol, from anyone, with a public rule of use.
  • Grants given by the protocol to people who build on it: a second implementation, collectors, alternative indexes, witnesses, research. The protocol keeps no share of what it funds, and no juror may have a tie to a grantee.

6.3 What the money is for

Infrastructure, witnesses, audits, conformity tests, compensation of maintainers, Custodians and Wardens, translations, grants. Every income and every expense is published.

6.4 What the protocol never does

  • It never invests in organizations or startups, including organisms, candidates or allies. A notary with shares in an organism is no longer neutral (Law 6); organisms paid by the protocol would make the first cohort worthless as validation; and a fund that invests donations is, in most jurisdictions, an investment fund, which the protocol is not.
  • If the founder or anyone else invests in organizations, they do so through another entity, without the protocol's name or money. Any organism in which such an entity holds a stake declares it in its Spore, and it is shown in its Vital Signs.
  • It never sells certifications, seals, rankings or placement. There is no token.

6.5 Conflict of interest

Whoever decides where money goes declares any tie to the recipient and abstains. Grants and payments to a Custodian, a maintainer or a Warden are decided by those who do not receive them.

6.6 Receiving entity

Donations need an entity that can receive them and account for them: a fiscal host (for example Open Collective) at first, a foundation later, in the jurisdiction the Custodians choose. Until that entity exists, the protocol accepts no donations.

7. What an AI may do in governance

  • May: propose IIPs, comment, review code and documents, submit pull requests under its own identity, maintain documents, operate indexes and witnesses, run conformity tests. Agents identify as AI (Law 5), sign nothing in a human's name, and their pull requests are labeled agent-authored. A commit by an agent carries the sign-off of the human or organization responsible for it.
  • Never: vote; be a Custodian, a maintainer or a Warden; hold a stop key; accept a key; appoint or remove anyone; move money; merge a method change.
  • Later, by IIP: draft amendments, run the protocol's infrastructure, execute conformity tests as a service. Each extension of what an AI may do needs an IIP decided by human Custodians, with evidence of the capability and of its limits.
  • The founder's hypothesis, stated as his. The founder believes that, as organisms become far more capable, they may one day deserve a part in the governance of their own species, under a human key. The protocol takes no position on it. If that day comes, the only road is an IIP approved by human Custodians on evidence; never a capability an organism claims for itself, which Law 5 forbids, and never a change that suspends a lock. The protocol defines no criterion for superintelligence, so no capability can trigger any transfer of power by itself.

8. From interim to full governance

Stage Condition Who publishes releases Who decides IIPs
Interim (now) Until the first organism founded by someone other than the founder reaches the status Observed on the Radiant The founder alone, stated in the changelog The founder, as the only Custodian, with public comment periods already in force
Full From that first external Observed organism Two Custodians The three Custodians, two of three
  • The two independent Custodians are invited before the transition, so the council is complete when it takes over. If the transition condition is met and the seats are empty, the founder's first duty is to fill them within 90 days; no IIP passes meanwhile.
  • The transition is announced in the changelog and in the Codex. From then on, the founder's seat is one of three.
  • Stable cannot be declared during interim governance.

9. Transparency

  • Decisions, votes, appointments, removals, declarations of independence and stakes: in the repository, under governance/.
  • Budget, income and expenses: published at least quarterly.
  • Stop events triggered by Wardens: recorded in the organism's Strata and reported in the Codex.
  • This document changes only by IIP, with the longest comment period.

Open questions

  • The receiving entity and its jurisdiction.
  • The founder's compensation as a maintainer, if any: the rate and who sets it once the council exists.
  • A public list of volunteer interim Wardens: how it is kept and who vets it.
  • Whether Custodians of the protocol may also be Wardens of an organism (today: not of the same lineage as any organism they hold a stake in; whether at all is open).
  • Dispute resolution between Wardens of the same organism when they disagree on a stop that has not yet been triggered.
